The Roots of the Petaluma Gap

Frank & Maria Flocchini

Long before our family's arrival, our soil carried a deep agricultural legacy. Our estate sits entirely within the historic 1834 boundaries of Rancho Petaluma—General Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo's sprawling 66,000-acre land grant. As this historic rancho transitioned into early California statehood and was subdivided, these pastures along the wind-swept Lakeville corridor lay waiting for their next chapter.

In 1911, a 20-year-old immigrant named Frank Flocchini arrived in California from a small village in Lombardia, Italy. Armed with nothing but an unrelenting work ethic, Frank navigated the challenges of a new country, establishing himself first as a regional share milker. Through years of rigorous saving and determination, he achieved his ultimate dream: purchasing the family's Lakeville farm on April 16, 1919.

Whether right or wrong, family lore remembers it as a risky gamble. When Frank bought the property, some locals openly laughed at the choice, dismissing it as terrible ground where the only thing that could grow well was star thistle. Nutrient-rich soil was nothing anyone saw in the acreage at the time.

Yet, where others saw a barren paddock, Frank saw a future. He returned to Italy to marry his wife, Maria, and together they returned to the ranch, raised five children, and proved the skeptics wrong. For decades, the property thrived as a traditional family dairy farm. Year after year, the land was meticulously cared for—including the decades of natural cow manure spread over the ground every autumn, quietly building a deep layer of organic richness.

The Core Foundation

Breaking the Clay Hardpan

When our family prepared to transition the historical pastures to premium vineyard acreage, we knew the land possessed extraordinary viticultural potential—but it required a foundational intervention. The Petaluma Gap's intense climate is matched by a challenging, dense subterranean clay hardpan that can easily restrict root development and water movement if left unaddressed.

Before a single length of irrigation pipe was laid, a single trellis row hammered in, or a single vine went into the ground, we executed an aggressive, multi-directional soil preparation program to open the clay hardpan.

Using a massive 6-foot single shank, we deep-ripped the entire estate matrix. The tractor ran a meticulous footprint: first tearing the ground in a criss-cross pattern and then following with an intensive catty-corner pass to shatter the hardpan completely. During this deep structural opening, gypsum was added directly into the sub-surface cuts to naturally alter the clay structure, accelerate drainage, and permanently unlock the lower mineral strata.

The Living Results: By permanently altering the hardpan down to six feet, we granted our young root systems an immediate, unrestricted path to dive deep into the cooler, lower soil profiles. Today, the difference in vine balance, resilience during hot spells, and flavor concentration is undeniable. Our vines don't struggle against a subterranean wall; they thrive deep within the earth, reflecting the raw, authentic character of the Petaluma Gap.

Stewardship

Regenerative & Sustainable Practices

Our just over 25 acres of premium estate vines are farmed with a deep commitment to regenerative viticulture. Rather than forcing inputs onto the land, we focus on closed-loop water stewardship and fostering a vibrant, living subterranean ecosystem to deliver fruit of exceptional purity, complexity, and structural character.

A Decade of No-Till Viticulture: Over 10 years ago, we committed to a strict no-till approach across our entire acreage. By leaving the soil completely undisturbed, we protect the complex underground networks of mycorrhizal fungi and beneficial microbes that traditional tilling destroys. This maintains natural structural integrity, vastly improving water retention and carbon sequestration.

The Biological Mowing Team: To manage our under-canopy cover crops, we integrate a resident flock of Dorper sheep into our seasonal vineyard rotation. These sheep act as a natural weed control team. As they graze, they naturally deposit organic nutrients back into the earth, inoculating the topsoil and drastically minimizing tractor mowing passes.

100% Herbicide-Free Matrix: No chemical herbicides, glyphosate, or synthetic weed killers touch our soil rows... ever. Cleaner soil yields cleaner, highly authentic juice, entering the winery as an ideal canvas for native, wild-ferment programs and low-intervention cellar practices.

100% Surface-Captured Rainwater Irrigation: To protect our local ecosystem and regional aquifers, we do not draw from local groundwater tables. Instead, our irrigation infrastructure relies entirely on a self-sustaining network of surface-captured rainwater collected and stored right on the ranch.

Terroir

The Clonal Specs & Block Allocations

Our blocks sit directly along the prestigious, wind-swept Lakeville corridor of the Petaluma Gap AVA. Rooted in rich Adobe and Goldridge clay, our micro-climate maximizes hang-time, yielding small berries with intense flavor concentration and exceptional natural acidity.

Because we believe this exceptional fruit is best expressed through the lens of focused, artisanal craftsmanship, we intentionally design our vineyard operations around small-lot flexibility.
