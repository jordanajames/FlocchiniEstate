Flocchini Estate Vineyards

Shaped by over a century of family farming along Petaluma's wind-swept Lakeville corridor, Flocchini Estate Vineyards grows limited-tonnage, cool-climate wine grapes for independent winemakers. Irrigated entirely with surface-captured rainwater and farmed 100% herbicide-free across 26 acres of estate vines.

Breaking the Clay Hardpan

At Flocchini Vineyards, we believe great wine is grown from the subsoil up. The Petaluma Gap's marine winds are matched underground by a dense clay hardpan that restricts root depth and water movement if left untouched.

Before a single length of irrigation pipe was laid or a single trellis post was driven, we ran a heavy 6-foot single ripper shank across the entire vineyard footprint—first tearing the ground in a criss-cross grid, then following with a diagonal catty-corner pass to shatter the clay pan completely. As we ripped, we banded gypsum directly into the six-foot cuts to open the clay structure, improve drainage, and unlock the lower mineral strata.

That early groundwork gave our vines a completely different start than neighboring properties planted on shallow ground. Our roots dive deep into cooler soil layers, resulting in natural canopy balance, resilience during heat spells, and concentrated fruit.

Regenerative & Sustainable Farming

Rather than forcing synthetic inputs onto the land, we focus on closed-loop water stewardship and active soil biology across every block.

A Decade of No-Till Viticulture

Our Stewardship: Over 10 years ago, we committed to a strict no-till approach across our entire acreage, leaving the soil undisturbed to protect native mycorrhizal fungi and beneficial microbes.

Winemaker's Advantage: Intact soil structure improves water retention, carbon sequestration, and natural nutrient cycling—translating directly into balanced canopies and complex flavor profiles.

The Grazing Team: 200 Dorper Sheep

Our Stewardship: We rotate a resident flock of 200 Dorper hair sheep through the vineyard during dormancy to graze cover crops and winter weeds.

Winemaker's Advantage: As the flock grazes, they cycle organic nutrients back into the topsoil, building diverse soil biology while drastically reducing heavy tractor mowing passes.

100% Herbicide-Free (Roundup-Free)

Our Stewardship: No chemical herbicides, glyphosate, or synthetic weed killers ever touch our vine rows.

Winemaker's Advantage: Cleaner soil yields cleaner juice. Our fruit enters the winery as an uncompromised, chemical-free canvas ideal for native, wild-ferment programs.

100% Surface-Captured Rainwater

Our Stewardship: To protect local aquifers, we do not pump from groundwater tables. Our irrigation relies entirely on winter rainwater collected and stored right on the ranch.

Winemaker's Advantage: Targeted deficit hydration is applied only when strictly necessary, encouraging deep roots, small berries, and natural vine balance.

Clonal Specs & Small-Lot Flexibility

Rooted in rich Adobe and Goldridge clay in the direct path of the Petaluma Gap wind corridor, our blocks benefit from extended hang-time that concentrates phenolics while preserving bright natural acidity.

We structure our operations around small-lot flexibility. Whether providing customizable 2-to-5-ton micro-lot allocations for independent winemakers running validation trials, or establishing dedicated block-level contracts, we work closely with buyers to match their cellar goals.

A Century on the Lakeville Ranch

Our estate sits entirely within the historic 1834 boundaries of Rancho Petaluma—General Mariano Guadalupe Vallejo's 66,000-acre land grant. In 1911, a 20-year-old immigrant named Frank Flocchini arrived in California from a small village in Lombardia, Italy. Starting out as a regional share milker, Frank saved relentlessly until he purchased the family's Lakeville ranch on April 16, 1919.

When Frank bought the property, some locals openly laughed at the gamble, dismissing the heavy clay ground as fit only for star thistle. Where others saw a barren paddock, Frank saw a future. He returned to Italy to marry Maria, and together they returned to the ranch, raised five children—Madeline (Lena), James, Andrew, Nicholas, and Anna—and built a thriving family dairy.

For decades, natural cow manure was spread over the pastures every autumn, quietly building a deep layer of organic richness that later became the foundation for the vineyard blocks brothers Andrew and Nicholas Flocchini planted here.
